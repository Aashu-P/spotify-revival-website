BEFORE THE SILENCE

Some songs begin
with almost nothing.

For Clara, it was a borrowed microphone, a bedroom door, and the few quiet hours between the last bus home and her morning shift.

She hung a duvet over the door of her tiny Bristol flat. Outside, the rain kept time against the window. Inside, she recorded the song she had been carrying around for months. No studio. No audience. Just the feeling that maybe someone, somewhere, would understand.

She called it A Place to Begin.

THE NUMBER THAT NEVER MOVED

She kept checking.
Then she stopped.

She uploaded the recording and shared the link with a few friends. Days became weeks. The song slipped beneath the noise of everything else, and the number beside it stayed exactly where it had started.

“I thought the silence
was my answer.”— Clara Hayes

There was no dramatic goodbye. She checked a little less often. Wrote a little less often. Eventually, she packed the guitar away. A song made with all that hope became a link she no longer opened.

A DIFFERENT ENDING

The recording stayed.
Now it has you.

That is the idea behind The Lost Tracks Revival: a song’s play count should never get the final word. There might be someone out there waiting for exactly this melody, exactly these words.

A first listen is a small thing to give. For the person on the other side of the song, it can mean everything.
